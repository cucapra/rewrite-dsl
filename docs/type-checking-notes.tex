    \textbf{LHS} & \textbf{Checks?} & \textbf{Explanation}\\
    \toprule
    $a:i \p b:j$ & No & The LHS is rewritten as $a:w_a \p b:w_b\quad\mathtt{where}\quad w_a=i,\  w_b=j$, and the compiler cannot infer that $i=j$, so we can't guarantee that $w_a$ and $w_b$ are in the same equivalence class. \\
    $a:i \p b:i$ & Yes & The compiler translates it to $a:w_a \p b:w_b\quad\mathtt{where}\quad w_a = i,\  w_b = i$, thus $w_a$ and $w_b$ are in the same equivalence class. \\
    $a:i \p b:j\break\quad\mathtt{where}\quad i = j$ & Yes & Similarly to the previous case. LHS is rewritten as $a:w_a \p b:w_b\quad\mathtt{where}\quad w_a = i,\  w_b = j, i=j$, so $w_a$ and $w_b$ are in the same equivalence class. \\
    $a:i \p b:(i+1)$ & No & Gets translated as $a:w_a \p b:w_b\quad\mathtt{where}\quad w_a = i,\  w_b = i+1$. This won't type-check as $w_a \neq w_b$, so definitely $w_a$ and $w_b$ are not in the same equivalence class. \\
    $a:i \p b:j \break\quad\mathtt{where}\quad i = k,\ k = j$ & Yes & By transitivity. \\
    $a:i \p b:j \break\quad\mathtt{where}\quad i = j,\ i = j+1$ & ? & Should we reject this? How? \\
    \bottomrule
